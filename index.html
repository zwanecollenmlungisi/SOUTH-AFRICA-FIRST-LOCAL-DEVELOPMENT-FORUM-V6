Our Five Strategic Pillars

The SAFLDF Career Exhibition and all our programmes are institutionally anchored on five strategic pillars, ensuring every initiative contributes to holistic, sustainable development across South Africa.

Local Enterprise Development

Fostering SMMEs and local economic participation. On-site NYDA grant kiosks and preferential procurement of local service providers.

Skills Training Initiatives

Building occupational and vocational competencies via SETAs (Agri, MER, EW), artisan trade test guidance, and TVET college pathway mapping.

Community Infrastructure Projects

Supporting municipal and civil development through career streams in civil engineering, construction, water management, and municipal planning.

Girl Child Empowerment Agenda

Advancing gender equity and female leadership with dedicated mentorship zones, female-first application assistance, dignity kits, and a 55% minimum female participation target.

Governance & Compliance

Upholding institutional integrity and governance via the IDSC framework, Treasury-approved budgeting, and robust Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) protocols.

Girl Child Empowerment Agenda

A cross-cutting priority embedded across all provincial chapters. We recognize that rural female learners face compounded barriers nationwide – including cultural expectations, early pregnancy risks, domestic responsibilities, and limited access to female mentors in STEM and trade sectors.

1. Women in STEM & Trades Pavilions

Each provincial venue will feature a dedicated zone where female professionals from mining engineering, agricultural science, renewable energy, and ICT conduct panel discussions and mentoring.

2. Female-First Application Assistance

NSFAS and university application kiosks will prioritize female learners during peak morning hours across all provinces, ensuring dedicated support before general walk-ins.

3. Sanitary Dignity Kits & Transport

In partnership with the Dept. of Social Development and traditional authorities nationwide, all female attendees will receive dignity pack vouchers and subsidized transport.

4. Targeted Tracking & 55% Benchmark

Of the learners reached nationally, we commit to a minimum 55% female participation per province, with post-exhibition gender-disaggregated tracking.

"Empowering a girl child in South Africa is not just a social justice issue – it is an economic multiplier. Every female artisan, engineer, or entrepreneur we place reduces household poverty and shifts community norms for the next generation."

Compliance & Governance Framework

Partnering with Government Entities, Schools & Municipalities. SAFLDF is fully compliant with South African legislation. Government departments, municipalities, schools, and state-owned entities are required to adhere to the following legal and policy frameworks when entering into partnerships, funding agreements, or procurement contracts with us.

Act 1 of 1999

PFMA – Public Finance Management Act

Applicable to National and Provincial Government Departments. Governs financial management, budgeting, procurement, and reporting.

Act 56 of 2003

MFMA – Municipal Finance Management Act

Applicable to Municipalities. Prescribes budgeting, financial management, supply chain management, and performance reporting at the local government level.

Act 5 of 2000

PPPFA & Treasury Regulations

The Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act and associated National Treasury Regulations govern all procurement processes.

Act 4 of 2013

POPIA – Protection of Personal Information Act

Regulates the processing of personal data – including learner information, stakeholder details, and participant records.

Act 71 of 2008

Companies Act (NPC Registration)

SAFLDF is registered as a Non-Profit Company (NPC) in terms of the Companies Act, ensuring fiduciary governance and transparent financial reporting.

Income Tax Act

Section 18A (Tax-Deductible Status)

SAFLDF holds Section 18A status with SARS, allowing donors and partners to claim tax deductions for qualifying donations and contributions.

Act 97 of 1998

SDA – Skills Development Act

Provides the framework for the National Skills Fund (NSF), SETA learnerships, skills programmes, and the National Skills Authority.

Act 55 of 1998

EEA – Employment Equity Act

Promotes equal opportunity and fair treatment in employment, eliminating unfair discrimination.

Act 84 of 1996

SASA – South African Schools Act

Governs the South African schooling system. Aligns with SAFLDF's holistic learner development and SGB engagements.
